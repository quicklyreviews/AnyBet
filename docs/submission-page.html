Resolution cost is what keeps markets small

Prediction markets are gated by the price of settling them. A Polymarket-scale market needs an operator to define, monitor and resolve each question, so only questions worth that overhead get to exist.

On GenLayer, will BTC be up in five minutes is already covered several times over. What is unserved is everything below that line: will my flight be delayed, did the council approve the permit, will England win the test, the bet two friends want settled without having to trust each other.

The blocker was never the money plumbing — parimutuel pools are a solved problem. It is that settling an arbitrary question means reading evidence and exercising judgment, which is exactly what a deterministic chain cannot do and an oracle can only do by being trusted.

What GenLayer changes

Resolution stops being an operator function. A market carries its own criteria and its own sources; at close, every validator independently fetches those sources over the real internet and runs the same resolution prompt, and the transaction only lands if their verdicts agree.

Judgment becomes a consensus primitive. The marginal cost of one more market falls to roughly the cost of one more transaction — which is the thing that makes long-tail markets possible at all.

How a market runs

  1. Create. A question, the criteria that decide it, a close time, and up to three source URLs. No approval, no topic list, no admin.

  2. Bet. YES or NO, parimutuel — the pool minus a small fee splits across the winning side in proportion to stake.

  3. Resolve. Permissionless once the window closes. Validators each fetch, each run the prompt, and only a matching normalized outcome is accepted.

  4. Collect. Winners claim; one call sweeps every outstanding market at once.

Three outcomes, and the third one is the design

A market resolves to one of three states. The third is not an error path — it is the safety property the whole contract is built around.

YES

The evidence settles it. The pool pays the YES side, minus the fee.

NO

The evidence settles it the other way. The pool pays the NO side.

UNKNOWN

Absent, partial or ambiguous evidence. The market voids and every stake is refunded in full, no fee taken.

The resolution prompt is explicit about when to reach for the third: a wrong UNKNOWN only delays a market, while a wrong YES pays the wrong person.

A market also voids when one side never attracted a bet. There is no counterparty to win from, so taking a fee — or paying anyone out — would be taking money from a bet nobody opposed.

Why strict_eq is the wrong equivalence here

Strict equality works when validators reproduce byte-identical output. That is right for a price feed and wrong for a judgment: two honest model calls can word their reasoning quite differently while agreeing completely on the verdict.

AnyBet uses a custom leader/validator pair through gl.vm.run_nondet_unsafe that compares only the normalized outcome — the part that has to be identical for consensus to mean anything — and lets the reasoning vary freely. The reasoning is still stored on-chain and shown in the UI, so a settlement stays auditable without demanding unanimous prose.

Verified on StudioNet

Not a claim — a run. Full lifecycle on-chain with nothing mocked: deposit, bets on both sides, LLM resolution through validator consensus, parimutuel payout, claim.

One GEN in the pool, three per cent to the treasury, and 0.97 GEN to the side that was right — correct to the wei.

The run we are proudest of is the one that failed

The first live resolution came back UNKNOWN.

Binance geo-blocks GenLayer's validator nodes — and does it with HTTP 200 and an error body, so code that trusted the status line would have parsed a price out of an error page. Instead the validators agreed on UNKNOWN, the market voided, and every stake stayed refundable in full.

When the evidence is not there, the system refuses to decide rather than paying the wrong side. A demo that only ever shows the happy path has not shown this.

That failure also produced a tool: probe_sources.py deploys a throwaway fetch-and-report contract, so any candidate source can be checked against what validators actually receive in about eighty seconds, instead of guessing through a six-minute market cycle. CoinGecko, Coinbase and Kraken all pass.

A book with real people in it

Thirty-four independent StudioNet wallets have bet across four markets on this contract — every one a real account with its own key, every bet a real transaction, stakes drawn at random between 1 and 5 GEN.

The interface can show that because the contract was changed to let it. The first version stored every bet but only handed one back at a time, and only to a caller who already knew which address to ask for — so nobody could see who was on which side. A market whose book cannot be read is a market you have to take on trust, which is the opposite of the point. get_market_bets() now returns the lot.

55 GEN we can never get back

Winnings here are recorded, not pushed: settlement fixes what each bet is owed, and the winner claims it. That is a deliberate choice — a win you press a button to take is a win you noticed — and it has a consequence we walked straight into.

The first three markets were seeded by a script that minted wallets in memory and dropped them when it exited. The contract had already recorded what each one was owed. Nothing can ever collect it, because claiming is signed by the winner and the winner no longer exists.

Still unclaimed across the contract: 55.112 GEN.

It is exactly the sum of the winning side of those three markets, and it is not recoverable by anyone, the contract owner included. The seeding script now keeps its wallets, and the fourth market's winners collected in full — the difference between the two figures is the whole argument for why that step exists, made at our own expense rather than in a footnote.

The app

No build step. Markets are readable without signing in; playing needs a wallet, and that is the only way in. Connecting adds StudioNet to the wallet if it has never seen it, and test GEN is one button away, so a judge goes from a cold browser to a placed bet in a couple of minutes.

An earlier build also offered a guest account — a throwaway key minted into localStorage. It made trying the app easier, and it was wrong: it handed people an account they were never told they had, so opening the site in a second browser left the money in the first one unreachable. That happened to us during development. A prediction market should not ship that as its default, so it is gone; the page holds no keys at all and the wallet signs everything.

The account panel names the network the wallet is actually on — StudioNet in green, anything else in amber beside a switch button — and a write on the wrong chain is refused up front rather than signed and left to fail a minute later.

Two things the interface takes care not to lie about. A one-sided market is quoted as a refund, not as odds, because a multiplier derived from the only funded pool would advertise a payout below 1× that the contract can never make. And “voided” always says which kind: Voided — YES, unopposed is a different event from Voided — undecidable, and the first build of the UI reported the second as the first, which was simply untrue.

Findings worth passing back

Three things cost real time and are not in the documentation.

Contract time

Use datetime.datetime.now(), never time.time(). genvm-lint raises W002 on the latter and accepts the former, and gltest's VM implements vm.warp() by patching datetime.datetime — so time.time() is untestable as well as non-deterministic. Confirmed on-chain.

Deploying with genlayer-py

Contract source must be pure ASCII. The SDK hex-encodes it through eth_utils.encode_hex, which is ASCII-only, so a single em dash in a comment breaks schema fetching — and it fails after a successful deploy, surfacing as a misleading “Failed to get schema from all clients”. genlayer-js has no such limit.

Web access from validators

Binance is unreachable from validator nodes and answers with a 200 and an error body. CoinGecko, Coinbase and Kraken all work.
