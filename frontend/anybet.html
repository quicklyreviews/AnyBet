Resolution cost is what keeps markets small

A Polymarket-scale market needs an operator to define, monitor and settle every question, so only questions worth that overhead get to exist. On GenLayer, will BTC be up in five minutes is already covered several times over.

What is unserved is everything below that line: will my flight be delayed, did the council approve the permit, will it rain on the wedding, the bet two friends want settled without having to trust each other.

The blocker was never the money. It is that settling an arbitrary question means reading evidence and exercising judgment — exactly what a deterministic chain cannot do, and what an oracle can only do by being trusted.

Judgment as a consensus primitive

A market carries its own criteria and its own sources. At close, every validator independently fetches those sources over the real internet and runs the same resolution prompt — and the transaction only lands if their verdicts match. The marginal cost of one more market falls to about the cost of one more transaction, which is what makes long-tail markets possible at all.

YES

The evidence settles it. The pool pays the YES side, minus the fee.

NO

The evidence settles it the other way. The pool pays the NO side.

UNKNOWN

Absent, partial or ambiguous evidence. The market voids and every stake is refunded in full, no fee taken.

The third one is the design, not an error path. The prompt tells the resolver to reach for it whenever the evidence is thin, on the grounds that a wrong UNKNOWN only delays a market, while a wrong YES pays the wrong person.

Getting that consensus is five lines of Python, and choosing the comparison correctly is the entire design problem — strict_eq is the obvious answer and the wrong one. The code is quoted here, alongside how to write against either contract from a script.

The run we are proudest of is the one that failed

The first live resolution came back UNKNOWN. Binance geo-blocks GenLayer's validator nodes — and does it with HTTP 200 and an error body, so code trusting the status line would have parsed a price out of an error page.

The validators agreed on UNKNOWN, the market voided, and every stake stayed refundable in full. That is the safety argument demonstrated in production rather than asserted in a README: when the evidence is not there, the system refuses to decide instead of paying the wrong side.
